# Act I — Siege mystery: truthful order, forged promise

**Status:** approved CORE REVEAL DIRECTION by user on 2026-10-19 ("второй" in response to two competing twists). Full story, culprit, oath, individual letters and ending mechanics are **not yet approved or implemented**. This is a narrative brief, not gameplay content.

## Locked premise (player choice)

- Long ago, the protagonist was a celebrated hero/strategic decision-maker in a war. Preserve protagonist identity as Knight, Ranger or Mage; do NOT canonize only a knight/general profession or rewrite class selection.
- An invaded country faced a catastrophic choice. Send forces to rescue a besieged castle and weaken the country's defenses, or deliberately leave the castle holding the invasion force while assembling reinforcements for a devastating counterattack elsewhere.
- The protagonist made the terrible strategic choice to **sacrifice the castle to save the country**. This moral responsibility remains real even if their communications were falsified.
- Critically, the protagonist **sent an honest original order**: reinforcements would not come; defenders were being asked to hold if they could, knowing the true cost. **Someone else replaced/forged that order** with messages falsely promising that help was on its way. The defenders and civilians waited for a rescue that never came.
- **The forger's identity and motive are OPEN mysteries**, not yet canon. Do not make the Wizard, commandant, king, messenger or any other person the culprit by default. The Wizard is aware of or exploits the history but his actual complicity remains open.
- The reason the castle garrison remains undead is also OPEN (a last-defense oath, magic, ritual or other cause is a candidate). Do not prematurely canonize a literal oath or explain all undead with one mechanism before player approval.
- The Act I mystery is **who changed the message, who knew about it, and whether informed defenders would have chosen to hold anyway**. Telling the truth does not absolve the protagonist of sacrificing the castle.

## Player experience and revelations (design proposals, not finalized plot)

1. First tier: scraps of hopeful civilian and garrison letters repeat "aid at dawn"; dead soldiers still guard stations.
2. Mid tier: incompatible dates, signatures, seals, treasury and tribunal entries hint that dispatches were tampered with. Let player examine tabletop objects, not only click cards.
3. Late tier: verify the original frank military order, and learn it was replaced. Preserve doubt about the forger's motive and what the castle's defenders would choose knowing the truth.
4. XIII Bone Warden: character-driven confrontation with the commander's choice, a surviving piece of evidence and a genuine moral choice. Whether the Warden once commanded the castle is **a proposal only**, not locked.
5. Ending concept: repeat the strategic sacrifice to save the country versus save the castle and endanger the country. A third informed/evacuation option may become possible through investigations but must involve a real price, not a free happy ending. Exact options, outcomes and scope of altered later acts need design approval.

## Roguelike and UI integration rules

- 12 Fate Spread resolutions plus sealed XIII; 39/45 live cards at this decision. Future order of cards stays unknown and randomized.
- Avoid a brittle requirement to draw specific 3 cards in one run. Several cards must each be able to communicate key facts; clues can be found in different combinations and persist only as explicitly designed.
- Reading should be optional but meaningful. Clues can unlock extra questions, counterarguments, preparation or honest informed choices; do not punish missing lore with an incoherent ending.
- Use current RMB card-lore inspection and accepted DS2 tabletop style as an interface entry point. Potential tangible interactions: unfold a dispatch, compare wax seals, align two pieces of a letter, inspect the Wizard's discarded cards.
